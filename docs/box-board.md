## Why this board

On 2026-11-14 the pedal ran in its 1590DD case for the first time. Inside were vip (a Pi 5) on the
4.3″ DSI screen's standoffs, the SparkFun WM8960 board and a one-channel output board floating on
tape, a Pico, three footswitches, a rotary controller and a lighted button. The input buffer was
an external pedal, because no small enough buffer board existed.

This board replaces the codec board, the output board, the external buffer and the loose Pico
wiring with **one board that is mounted, not floating**. It carries the circuit the phase 2
schematic already has (the TAC5212, its supplies, the instrument buffer, the mic preamp, the
differential drivers into the codec, the output stage and its mute), cut down to what the box
needs and reshaped to the space the box leaves.

The full-size HAT drawn in phase 2 (two combo inputs, balanced outputs, phantom, a panel of
switches) is a different product: a pedal in a bigger case. It stays in git history (`a67618f`)
as the source the box board's sheets are cut from, and as the starting point if the bigger case
comes back.

## Requirements

### Form and mounting

- **R1. One board, mounted to the case's base plate** on standoffs, so it comes away with the
  plate and nothing on it floats. The base plate is the 1590DD's removable lid, so opening the
  case lifts the board out with every connector still reachable.
- **R2. It lies flat, parallel to the Pi and the screen, under the footswitches.** The brief's
  first estimate is about **170 × 25 mm**: the case's full width, and from the front wall to the
  Pi's front edge (y ≈ 67 to 101). The outline has to clear the corner pillars and the
  front-centre pillar (x ≈ 89), so it is a strip with a notch, not a rectangle. Phase 3 measures
  this; until then these figures are estimates.
- **R3. It may extend into the right-hand region** (x ≈ 105–170, y ≈ 35–67, the WM8960's area
  today) if the strip is too small, with holes cut for the rotary controller's and the button's
  bodies. That region is also under the rear jacks' bodies, so only low parts go there.
- **R4. Height.** Allowing for the floor, the standoffs and the board, the strip leaves about
  16 mm above the board under the footswitches. That is the footswitches' 10 mm and some
  clearance, out of the case's 33. Phase 3 measures it. Every part, and every connector with its
  plug in, stays under it.
- **R5. The Pi connects by a short 40-way ribbon (IDC).** The board carries a shrouded, keyed
  2 × 20 box header at its edge nearest the Pi's header. The ribbon carries power, I2S, I2C, the
  ID EEPROM's bus and the UART. The board is no longer physically a HAT, but it keeps the HAT+ ID
  EEPROM and HAT+'s standby rules, so the Pi still identifies it and loads its overlay.

### Connections

- **R6. Everything off the board is on a JST XH connector.** Jacks, switches, the encoder and the
  button all come in on wires; nothing is mounted to the board through the case, so no plug or
  stamp strains it. Pairs are twisted and the input and output runs kept apart, as now.

| Connector | XH | Pins |
|---|---|---|
| GUITAR IN | 3-pin | sleeve, tip, the jack's switching contact (plug detect) |
| MIC IN | 3-pin | ground (XLR 1 and the ¼″ sleeve), hot (XLR 2 and tip), cold (XLR 3 and ring) |
| OUT | 3-pin | sleeve, tip (left), ring (right) |
| FS1, FS2, FS3 | 2-pin each | switch, ground |
| EXT FS | 3-pin | sleeve, tip (switch A), ring (switch B) |
| MIDI IN | 3-pin | the 3.5 mm TRS socket's tip (current source, DIN 4) and ring (sink, DIN 5), and its sleeve (shield, DIN 2), which a MIDI input leaves unconnected |
| ENCODER | 5-pin | in the KY-040's own header order: CLK, DT, SW, + (3.3 V), GND |
| BUTTON | 4-pin | NO, common, LED +, LED − (the NC contact is unused) |
| Pi | 2 × 20 box header | the 40-way ribbon |

### Audio

- **R7. One guitar input**, on the rear jack (a switching jack, so the board knows when a plug is
  in), through the instrument path phase 1 designed: 1 MΩ
  to the mid-rail, a follower, then the differential driver into the codec's input 1. Line-level
  sources work on the same input up to its full scale, +6.1 dBu, which a separate Line mode would
  not improve on enough to be worth a switch. **No mode relays**: one jack, one path.
- **R8. One mic input**, on the right-hand wall, near the guitar input and away from the output,
  through phase 1's mic preamp (about 20 dB, OPA1612) into the codec's input 2. **The socket is a
  combo XLR/¼″** (decided): it reserves the space the biggest option needs, so if the wall turns
  out too low for it, the fallback takes less room, never more. Both halves are wired in parallel
  (XLR 2 and the tip hot, XLR 3 and the ring cold, XLR 1 and the sleeve ground), so a balanced
  mic on either half, or an unbalanced one on the ¼″, reaches the same differential preamp.
  **No phantom power** (decided): the input is for dynamic mics and mics that power
  themselves.
- **R9. A stereo output** on the existing TRS jack: left on the tip, right on the ring, each from
  phase 1's unbalanced output stage. A mono (TS) plug shorts the ring to the sleeve, so the right
  channel has to drive a dead short indefinitely without harm or audible effect on the left. Its
  100 Ω series resistor and the OPA1656's current limit are checked for that.
- **R10. A bypass at rest** (decided). One DPDT relay, de-energised at rest, serves both
  channels. At rest one pole joins the guitar jack's tip to the output's tip, and the other holds
  the right channel at ground through a resistor. So the pedal passes the dry guitar while it
  boots, in the Pi's standby, and if anything fails. Energised, the poles take the left and right
  output stages instead. The RP2350 drives it. It energises the relay only once the Pi reports
  audio running over the UART, and drops it when the Pi's reports stop, because the RP2350 stays
  powered through the Pi's standby and reboots. The layout keeps the bypass's trace from the input
  end to the output short and guarded, since it carries the raw 1 MΩ signal.
- **R11. No balanced outputs, no pad, no panel switches, no XLR output**: the box has no room for
  them. The phase 2 sheets for them are dropped.

### Controls (the RP2350)

- **R12. The Pico's chip moves onto the board** (decided): an RP2350 with its QSPI flash, crystal,
  core regulator and 3.3 V supply, placed by JLCPCB, rather than a Pico module. It needs about a
  quarter of the area, and it has no radio (the pedal's wireless is the Pi's). Its USB pins go to
  test pads, for the bootloader on the bench. Everything it serves comes in on the board's XH
  connectors: FS1–FS3, EXT FS, MIDI IN, the encoder, the button and the bypass relay. It talks
  to the Pi over the ribbon's UART (GPIO 14/15, as now). This keeps the pedal's rule that physical controls belong to
  the Pico and that footswitch and MIDI events become identical UART messages
  (`../pedal.project/AGENTS.md`).
- **R13. The Pi can reset and program the RP2350 through the ribbon** (RUN, SWDIO and SWCLK to three
  Pi GPIOs), so a firmware update never needs a USB cable inside a closed case.
- **R14. The RP2350 runs from the Pi's 5 V**, through its own 3.3 V regulator, so it stays up
  through a Pi reboot and standby. Its signals to the Pi's 3.3 V pins are series-resistor or
  buffer isolated, so it can't back-power the Pi's GPIO while the Pi is in standby (HAT+).
- **R15. MIDI in** on a 3.5 mm TRS socket (MIDI Type A, decided; a DIN cable needs the usual
  adapter), through an optocoupler (6N138 or H11L1 class) into an RP2350 UART at 31 250 baud,
  with the socket's ground left floating, as the MIDI specification requires. That keeps the
  controller's ground out of the audio ground. MIDI out and thru are not needed.
- **R16. An external dual footswitch** on a ¼″ TRS jack: tip A, ring B, sleeve ground, the
  convention of the common two-button switches. Both are RP2350 inputs with pull-ups and RC
  debounce, the same as the onboard footswitches.
- **R17. The lighted button's LED** runs from 5 V (its built-in resistor suits 3–6 V), switched on
  the low side by a transistor from an RP2350 pin, and driven DC: on or off, or PWM'd well above
  audio (≥ 100 kHz), never at a few hundred Hz, because it sits beside the guitar input. **The
  encoder module is powered from 3.3 V**, not 5 V: its 10 kΩ pull-ups go to its + pin, and the
  RP2350's pins aren't 5 V tolerant. If the module's SW pull-up isn't fitted, as on many KY-040s,
  the board supplies one. The encoder's and switches' edges are RC-filtered at the connector.

### Noise and placement

- **R18. The board is arranged by signal.** The guitar and mic inputs are at its right-hand end,
  under their jacks. The codec and the analogue supply are right of centre. The output connector
  is near the output jack (x ≈ 120). The ribbon header, the RP2350 and the controls are at the
  left. The 1 MΩ node is short and guarded, with ground pour around it and nothing digital
  crossing beneath it.
- **R19. At least 30 mm between the input connectors and the output connector** on the board,
  and their cable runs kept apart in the case. Today's 38 mm between the jacks works.
- **R20. Footswitch, MIDI and encoder wires never cross the analogue end.** FS3 sits above the
  analogue end, so its connector goes at the strip's analogue/digital boundary, its wires routed
  along the front wall.
- **R21. One ground plane**, with analogue and digital kept apart by placement, not a split, as
  phase 3 already planned. **The case is bonded to circuit ground at one point**, chosen and then
  measured (decided): one bond near the inputs, and phase 8 compares it with none and with one
  elsewhere.
- **R22. MIDI and EXT FS sockets only if they fit without crowding the inputs** (the owner's
  condition). Both carry DC or isolated signals, so noise is not what limits them; the space for a
  jack's body inside the case is. Phase 3 finds out. The board carries their connectors either
  way, so the sockets can be added later.

## Power

As the phase 2 schematic, less what the box drops:

| Rail | From | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3 V AVDD | Pi 5 V, LC filter, TPS7A2033 | Codec AVDD |
| 3.3 V IOVDD | Pi 3.3 V (over the ribbon) | Codec IOVDD, EEPROM, logic pull-ups |
| Analogue 5 V | Pi 5 V, TPS22917 load switch, 10 µH and 110 µF | Buffer, preamp, FDAs, output stage, mid-rail |
| Relay coil | Pi 5 V, own filter branch | The one mute/bypass relay |
| RP2350 3.3 V | Pi 5 V, its own LDO (the RP2350's core regulator from that) | The RP2350, its flash, the control inputs' pull-ups, the encoder module |
| Button LED | Pi 5 V, switched | The button's LED ring |

The worst case is about 250 mA from the Pi's 5 V: op-amps ≈ 60 mA, the codec ≈ 30 mA, one relay
coil ≈ 30 mA, the RP2350 ≈ 30 mA, the button's LED ≈ 20 mA, and margin. The ribbon has two 5 V
conductors and seven grounds, which is plenty.

## Decided (owner, 2026-11-14)

1. **The mic socket is a combo XLR/¼″**, so the space inside is planned for the largest option
   (R8).
2. **No phantom power** on the box board (R8). It can come back on the bigger case's board.
3. **Bypass at rest**, released by the RP2350 once the Pi's audio runs (R10).
4. **The guitar jack is a switching jack**; the board reads its plug detect (R7).
5. **The RP2350 goes on the board as a chip**, not a Pico module (R12).
6. **One case bond to circuit ground, near the inputs**, measured in phase 8 (R21).
7. **MIDI in is a 3.5 mm TRS socket**, Type A, if there is room for it (R15, R22). This changes
   the pedal's D6, which said DIN, for this case.
8. **The controls:** the encoder is a KY-040 module, the knob The Pi Hut's 35 mm scrubber, and the
   button The Pi Hut's 16 mm rugged metal pushbutton with green LED ring (the table above, R17).

The B2B-XH-A and B3B-XH-A, the G6K-2F-Y DC5 (now one relay, the bypass), the 2N7002 (the
relay's and the button LED's drivers) and the 1N4148W are already in phase 2's list. The
RP2350's 0402 capacitors (4.7 µF, 100 nF) and resistors (33 Ω, 1 kΩ, 27 Ω) are JLCPCB basic parts.

**Why these.**
- **The core regulator follows Raspberry Pi's design exactly.** The RP2350's core supply is an
  on-chip switching regulator. Raspberry Pi's *Hardware design with RP2350* says its layout needs
  their inductor, the right way round, beside their 4.7 µF capacitors and the 33 Ω / 4.7 µF filter
  on VREG_AVDD, and that other choices are at the designer's risk. Feeding the core from an
  external linear regulator instead has been done, but Raspberry Pi doesn't document it, so the
  board copies the Pico 2's regulator layout. It keeps the regulator at the board's digital end,
  furthest from the inputs. Its switching frequency is far above audio. Phase 8 measures whether
  any of it reaches the inputs.
- **The crystal is the one Raspberry Pi recommends**, with its 1 kΩ damping resistor: the RP2350's
  oscillator circuit is tuned for it. JLCPCB's basic 12 MHz part (C9002) is 20 pF, not 10 pF, and
  would need the circuit re-tested over temperature.
- **The H11L1, not the 6N138.** The 6N138 needs 4.5 V; the H11L1 runs from 3 V, so it works from
  the RP2350's 3.3 V with no level shifting, and its Schmitt output gives clean edges at
  31 250 baud.
- **2 MB of flash** is plenty for the controls' firmware. The SOIC-8 alternative has more stock if
  the USON part runs short.
- **The ribbon's own parts** are the owner's to buy: 40-way grey ribbon at 1.27 mm pitch, and two
  2 × 20 IDC sockets (LCSC C21856 has them, with strain reliefs), at the length phase 3 measures.

**The sockets in the case** are not on the board, and none is a JLCPCB part. They are chosen once
phase 3 has measured the walls, from a UK distributor (CPC, as the pedal's other parts were):

- the mic input: a Neutrik combo XLR/¼″ socket with solder terminals or pins that take flying
  leads, about 26 × 31 mm on the panel;
- MIDI in: a panel-mount 3.5 mm stereo socket;
- the external footswitch: a panel-mount ¼″ stereo socket, enclosed, like the guitar jack;
- the output (stereo already) and the guitar input (a switching jack) stay as they are.

### What they mean for the fit

- **The strip is there.** From the ribbon plug to the front wall is 45 mm, so the board is about
  157 × 39 mm (x 10.5–167, y 68–107) with a notch for the front-centre pillar: about 60 cm²
  against about 45 cm² of parts.
- **Heights.** With the board's top 4.6 mm above the floor (3 mm standoffs, 1.6 mm board), a
  footswitch at its minimum 12 mm leaves 13.4 mm above the board; at 22 mm it leaves 3.4 mm,
  which is no room for parts, or for the switch's lugs and wires. A middle setting of about
  16 mm leaves about 9 mm: low surface-mount parts under the switches, nothing tall. Going to the
  full 22 mm needs a cut-out in the board under each switch, about 3 cm² each, which then
  wants parts on both sides or the right-hand extension.
- **The ribbon header cannot be directly in front of the Pi's plug.** It is 58 mm long, FS1 and
  FS2 are 69 mm apart, and mated with its socket it stands about 14 mm, taller than the space
  under a footswitch. It goes in front of the footswitch row (x ≈ 24–82, y ≈ 91–100), with the
  50.8 mm ribbon lying flat on the board between FS1 and FS2. That works if the footswitch
  bodies are no more than about 16 mm across, or sit high enough for the ribbon to pass under
  their edges.
- **Button B2 collides with the input jack where it was asked for** (the jack's body is
  right under the face out to y = 28). At 15 mm across it clears the jack from y ≈ 36, which
  is about 41 mm from the rear outside edge.
- **The mic combo socket fits the right-hand wall only just.** Turned so its flange is 31 mm
  along the wall and 26 mm in height (the case is 30 mm deep inside), it takes y ≈ 45–76 and
  reaches to x ≈ 147. That leaves about 2 mm to B2 behind it and about 4 mm to FS3 in front,
  and it needs a notch in the board's right-rear corner (about 20 × 11 mm). There is then no room on that wall for another
  socket.
- **MIDI between the rear jacks** works as a 3.5 mm socket (x ≈ 146, reaching about 14 mm in,
  short of the KY-040). **EXT FS has no place yet**: a ¼″ socket reaches 28 mm in, which fits
  nowhere on the rear or right walls. The choices are a second 3.5 mm socket between the rear
  jacks (about 14 mm pitch, tight for fat ¼″ plugs either side), a socket on the left wall of the
  front strip, or leaving it off this board.
- **Nothing goes under the rear jacks or the KY-040**: they come to within 4 mm of the floor,
  so a right-hand extension of the board would stay in front of y ≈ 45 and would be cut around
  the mic socket. The strip alone is the plan.

## Out of scope

The fans and their mounting, ventilation, the screen, the Pi, and the case's drilling. They are
the pedal's (`pedal.project`). The case drawing in `pedal.project` gains this board's outline,
the encoder, the button and the jacks in phase 3, because that is where the fit is judged.
